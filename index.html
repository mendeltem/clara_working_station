Alita · local agent, no network

What is computed on this machine, and what of it is published. A workbench, not a portfolio: only what was produced here is listed.

Workflow

One request, read left to right, using "I want to create a WMH segmentation model, can you research it?" as the example:

  1. Prompt. The researcher states the goal in plain language.
  2. Research agents. Clé (Charité API) splits the question and sends several agents to search papers and code for every method and architecture used for the task.
  3. Method shortlist. The findings are merged into candidate methods, such as 2D U-Net, 3D U-Net, nnU-Net or BIANCA, each turned into tasks with machine-checkable acceptance criteria.
  4. Harness, outer loop. Takes one method at a time from the queue and moves on once it is done.
  5. Harness, inner loop on the workstation GPU. Clara, the local model, writes the code, trains on open data (the WMH Segmentation Challenge training set), and an acceptance check counts the facts. If the check fails, the task goes back to Clara. The check asks no model.
  6. Evaluation. Every model is scored on the challenge test set with the official metric (Dice, lesion F1) and placed next to the public leaderboard, where the 2017 winner reached Dice 0.80.
  7. Result. The best model, a comparison table and a QC page.

Clara sees the data and has no network access. Clé and the research agents have network access and never see the data.

Why a local workstation

Shared GPU time is too slow for this kind of work. On the institute cluster, a job asking for a whole GPU was scheduled about 9 hours ahead; only 7 of the cards have 80 GB. A small share of a card starts within seconds, but it only fits small trainings (measurement, 16 Oct 2026). An agent that builds a model, trains it, finds an error and tries again needs dozens of such rounds a day. With a queue in between, each round costs hours; on the workstation it costs minutes.

A local model can look at the patient data; a cloud model cannot. Clara runs on the workstation, so the images never leave the machine. That lets her do what a remote model may not: open every scan, read every mask, run the model on the whole cohort, and look at the cases where it fails. She sees far more of the data than any summary would carry, and she sees it fast.

Local is a route, not a permission. Whether a dataset may be processed by an AI system at all is decided by its data agreement, and a clause that forbids it binds the local model too (rule). Where processing is allowed, running it locally keeps the data inside the institution.

The system behind it

Tools, measurements and operating rules are kept in local_models, including the model-independent scaffolding that describes what lets an agent finish a task. What the agents learned along the way is collected in the wiki blackbeard_wikki.

  • RTX A5000 with 24 GB (Ampere), i9-12900K, 62 GiB RAM, Ubuntu 24.04, NVIDIA driver 550 (CUDA 12.4). As of October 2026.
  • Clara's model is Qwen3.8-27B in Q4 via llama.cpp. Against Nemotron 3.5 Bolt 74B-A9B on the same card: 100 % vs 88.5 % on a 13-task test. Bolt generates 66–69 tok/s against about 30, but reasons longer. Entry
  • The model server unloads when idle: 21,770 MiB VRAM in use, 1,618 MiB after, 11.4 s to wake. This keeps the card free for training.

What is not here. Patient data and results on in-house cohorts stay on the machine. Only work on public datasets is published: the QC above runs on the WMH Segmentation Challenge (Utrecht, Singapore, Amsterdam). Checked before every push: every dataset needs an explicit release. Anything derived from in-house data inherits its class (rule), so the comparisons on the mixed pool of challenge and in-house cohorts are not shown here.
